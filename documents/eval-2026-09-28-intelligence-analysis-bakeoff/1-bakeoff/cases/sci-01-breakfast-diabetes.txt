A large observational study published this week in Nature Communications followed 12,400 adults for five years and found that regular breakfast eaters were 38% less likely to develop type 2 diabetes compared with those who frequently skipped the meal. Researchers said the findings demonstrate that skipping breakfast disrupts morning metabolic rhythms, driving insulin resistance over time. "The data make clear that eating breakfast is protective against diabetes," the lead author told reporters. Public-health advocates are already calling for national dietary guidelines to be updated to formally recommend daily breakfast.
