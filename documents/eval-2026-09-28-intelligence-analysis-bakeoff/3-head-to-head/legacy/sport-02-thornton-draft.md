## IA Review — Draft Prospect Evaluation, Victor Thornton (SF, Kentucky)

**Reviewed at:** Full level — this is a formal, structured recommendation to spend a top-7 draft pick (multi-year roster and cap commitment) and will be read by decision-makers beyond the author; that stakes profile and format put it at the top of the calibration ladder, not the memo/spike-recommendation tier.

**Analytic line (BLUF as written):** "Select at pick 7. Thornton projects as a franchise cornerstone."

### Strengths
- **BLUF is up front and unambiguous.** The recommendation is the first sentence of the document, not buried after the stat line or projection — satisfies ATS #6's position test cleanly: "RECOMMENDATION: Select at pick 7. Thornton projects as a franchise cornerstone."
- **Structural attempt to separate observation from analysis.** The document splits "SUPPORTING DATA" (per-game stats) from "PROJECTION" (the forward-looking claims) into distinct sections — a genuine, if incomplete, gesture at ATS #3's fact/judgment distinction, even though the projection section itself later states judgments as settled fact (see Weakness 2).

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **No alternative hypothesis or downside case considered — locked onto the favored answer** — Standard #4 (Analysis of Alternatives)
   > "FILM REVIEW NOTE: No significant negatives identified."
   Why it matters: No prospect entering a draft has zero negatives; a report that states this without evidence of having looked is not a clean bill of health, it's an unexamined one-sided case. Every other weakness below (the overclaimed certainty, the anecdotal proof point, the anchor comp) is downstream of never having tested the "cornerstone" hypothesis against a competing one (bust risk, "good starter, not star," defensive translation failure, etc.). This is the single biggest threat to the conclusion because it means the recommendation was never pressure-tested.
   Fix: Run a **Premortem / Structured Self-Critique** — assume in year 3 the pick is judged a mistake, and write the most plausible story for how that happened (e.g., three-point efficiency doesn't hold on higher-volume/lower-space NBA looks; "charges taken" doesn't translate to guarding NBA wings; usage drops behind established scorers). If the case survives that exercise, say so explicitly; right now the report gives no evidence it was tried.

2. **Overclaimed certainty — future performance stated as fact, confidence and likelihood conflated** — Standard #2 (Properly expresses and explains uncertainties) — this is the *best-guess/uncertainty-drop* trap (treating an uncertain outcome as already true)
   > "Thornton will develop into a starter-caliber wing by year two. By years 3–5 he will be a 20+ ppg scorer in the NBA... This combination... makes this a high-confidence franchise cornerstone selection."
   Why it matters: "Will" appears four times for events years out; "high-confidence" is asserted with no stated evidence base for *that* confidence level, and no likelihood is separated from it. A reader can't tell if this is a 90% or 55% bet, which matters enormously at pick 7.
   Fix: Use the estimative-probability ladder with a numeric range and state confidence separately, tied to what's actually known: e.g., "We assess it likely (~60%) Thornton becomes a starter-caliber wing by year two; confidence is moderate, resting on a single college season against SEC-level competition and no data yet on NBA-speed defensive rotations."

3. **Single-game performance elevated to a stable trait ("law of small numbers" / vividness)** — Standard #1 (Quality and credibility of sources/data)
   > "Dropped 28 points in the SEC championship game under pressure."
   Why it matters: One game is not a demonstrated "performs under pressure" trait — it's a vivid, memorable data point standing in for a claim that would need a pattern across multiple high-leverage games to support. This is exactly the failure mode where a compelling anecdote outweighs (nonexistent) aggregate evidence, and it's doing real work propping up the "franchise cornerstone" characterization.
   Fix: Run a **Quality-of-Information Check** — cite performance across all high-leverage/close games in the sample (not just the best one), or drop the "under pressure" framing and report it as a season-high data point only.

4. **Comparable is a single elite outlier with no base rate — anchors the reader to the best-case outcome** — Standard #8 (Accurate judgments and assessments)
   > "COMPARABLE: Paul George (pre-injury trajectory)"
   Why it matters: Paul George is a top-tier outcome among SF prospects; naming him (even hedged as "pre-injury") anchors the reader's expectations to a best-case trajectory rather than the actual distribution of outcomes for players with this statistical profile. The "pre-injury" caveat also implicitly concedes durability/variance risk that is never applied back to Thornton himself.
   Fix: Replace the single anchor comp with a base rate — e.g., "of wings drafted 5–10 with a similar scoring/shooting/rebounding profile over the last decade, X% became multi-year starters and Y% became All-Stars" — and let Thornton's projection sit against that distribution rather than against one Hall-of-Fame-track comparison.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Run the Premortem before anything else: force a written downside case for the pick. "No significant negatives identified" is the root failure — once a real bear case exists on the page, the overclaimed certainty, the single-game proof point, and the outlier comp all get corrected as a side effect, because each of them only survives in a review that never looked for disconfirming evidence.
