## IA Review — Africa Corps Expansion and Sahel Junta Cohesion (Intelligence Briefing, May 2025)

**Reviewed at:** Full level — this is styled and structured as a formal intelligence assessment (a discrete ASSESSMENT section carrying a forecast in ICD-203 calibrated language, plus a COLLECTION BASIS/sourcing statement), covering a threat/forecast topic named explicitly in the Full-level list, and built to be relied on by a policy-facing reader rather than read once and discarded like a short update.

**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "This trajectory will very likely result in Africa Corps establishing a permanent forward logistics hub in Mali within 12 months, after which Africa Corps will expand operations into Niger and subsequently provide Russia a strategic platform to contest both French and US access across the entire Sahel corridor."

### Strengths
- Honest about a corroboration gap instead of papering over it: "Niger figures unconfirmed" is flagged rather than folded into the same confidence as the Mali/Burkina Faso ranges — partial credit toward properly describing the quality and credibility of sources.
- Uses an actual ICD 203 calibrated estimative term ("very likely") rather than an undefined hedge like "probably" or "could" — the right vocabulary, even though its application is flawed (see Weakness 3).

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **Absence of pushback treated as proof of stability** — Standard #3, properly distinguishes underlying information from analysts' assumptions and judgments (also Standard #6, logical argumentation)
   > "No significant ECOWAS pressure or credible external intervention has materialized to reverse junta consolidation, which confirms that AES governments have achieved durable domestic stability."
   Why it matters: the entire forward-looking chain (permanent hub → Niger expansion → strategic platform against France/US) is built on the premise that the AES bloc is cohesive and stable. "No one has successfully pushed back" does not logically confirm "the governments are durably stable" — there are other live explanations (external actors preoccupied elsewhere, lacking capability, or deterred for unrelated reasons) that the product never rules out. This is exactly the kind of hidden, load-bearing premise the library's reasoning check exists for.
   Fix: Run a Key Assumptions Check on "AES governments have achieved durable domestic stability" — state it explicitly as an assumption, not a confirmed judgment, list what would make it false, and check whether any of that has already happened (e.g., internal AES friction, economic strain) before letting the rest of the forecast rest on it.

2. **Single hypothesis, no competing explanation considered** — Standard #4, incorporates analysis of alternatives (explicitly required at Full level)
   > absent: no alternative trajectory, outcome, or explanation appears anywhere in the product — the piece moves directly from background to a single forecast chain.
   Why it matters: per the library's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses method, the most likely hypothesis is the one with the least evidence against it, not the most evidence for it — and that can't be assessed with only one hypothesis on the table. Plausible competitors go unexamined here: AES cohesion fracturing under economic or security strain, Africa Corps assets being pulled toward higher-priority theaters, or continued (if unsuccessful) external pressure changing the junta's calculus.
   Fix: Run at least a Light ACH pass — step 1 (name one real competing trajectory, e.g., "AES fragmentation stalls the hub" or "Russia deprioritizes the Sahel") and step 5 (ask whether the cited evidence — troop presence figures, absence of ECOWAS pressure — actually discriminates between that alternative and the stated forecast, or is equally consistent with both).

3. **A three-step forecast chain carries one probability term for the whole chain** — Standard #2, properly expresses and explains uncertainties; bias: Conjunction / scenario inflation
   > "will very likely result in Africa Corps establishing a permanent forward logistics hub in Mali within 12 months, after which Africa Corps will expand operations into Niger and subsequently provide Russia a strategic platform..."
   Why it matters: the library states plainly that the probability of a multi-step scenario is the product of its steps, not a single shared figure — three 70% steps compound to roughly 34%, not 70%. Here, "very likely" (80–95% on the ICD 203 ladder) is applied once to a chain of three sequential, individually uncertain events (hub established → Niger expansion follows → strategic platform against France/US results), which overstates confidence in the compound, most policy-relevant outcome. No confidence level (separate from likelihood) is stated at all.
   Fix: State a likelihood for each link, and note that the compound probability is necessarily lower than the first step's; add a separate confidence line (e.g., "confidence is moderate, resting on troop-presence estimates that are themselves unconfirmed for Niger") per the library's likelihood/confidence split, rather than one blended term for the whole scenario.

4. **Bottom line arrives after background, not before it** — Standard #6, uses clear and logical argumentation (BLUF)
   > The product opens with "BACKGROUND: Following the 2021–2023 coups in Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger, the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) expelled French forces and formalized security partnerships..." — a paragraph of chronology and troop-count context — before the judgment appears under "ASSESSMENT:".
   Why it matters: this is the disguised case the library names directly — a report that opens with background/numbers instead of the judgment. A reader who stops after the first paragraph gets coup history and troop estimates, not the answer. It doesn't make the judgment itself wrong, which is why it ranks behind the three substantive findings above, but for a product meant to be read once by a decision-maker, it is a real BLUF failure.
   Fix: Lead with the judgment — "Africa Corps will very likely establish a permanent logistics hub in Mali within 12 months and use it to expand into Niger, [confidence level]" — then follow with the coup history and troop figures as supporting context, not as a preamble.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Run a Key Assumptions Check on "AES governments have achieved durable domestic stability" before writing anything else — state it as an assumption rather than a confirmed judgment, and list what would falsify it. Every downstream step in the forecast (the hub, the Niger expansion, the strategic platform against France and the US) is built on top of that one premise, so it is the single highest-leverage thing to get right or retract.
