## IA Review — DRAFT PROSPECT EVALUATION — Victor Thornton, SF, Kentucky

**Reviewed at:** Full level — this is a finished, standalone evaluation built to be relied on for a specific, consequential, irreversible personnel decision (spending pick 7): it states a recommendation as a judgment, states an explicit confidence ("high-confidence franchise cornerstone selection"), and lays out supporting data, a multi-year forecast, a comparable, and a film-review finding for the reader to check. That structure — not the word "draft" in the title, which refers to the NBA draft, not to the document's finish state — is what places it at Full rather than Medium.

**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "Select at pick 7. Thornton projects as a franchise cornerstone."

### Strengths
- The bottom line is up front, not buried under background or chronology — the very first line is the recommendation itself, satisfying the position rule bound up in Standard #6 (clear and logical argumentation).
- It directly answers the decision-maker's actual question — a specific pick, a specific player — rather than a general opinion of Thornton's talent, satisfying Standard #5 (customer relevance).
- The supporting data is genuinely reported information, not pure impression: concrete, specific per-game and shooting numbers (22.4 ppg, 7.2 reb, 3.1 ast; 41.2% on 6.2 3PA) rather than adjectives alone — real material for Standard #3's "information" leg, even though how it's labeled is a separate problem flagged below.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **No alternative considered — a single hypothesis dressed as a finding** — Standard 4, Incorporates analysis of alternatives
   > "FILM REVIEW NOTE: No significant negatives identified."
   Why it matters: a pick-7 recommendation with an explicit claim of *zero* negatives, for a rookie prospect, is the clearest possible signature of only one explanation ever being on the table. Nowhere does the product weigh a bust scenario, a translation risk (does 6.2 three-point attempts/41.2% survive against longer, faster NBA defenders?), or any competing read of the same stats. This directly undercuts the "high-confidence" label the recommendation rests on.
   Fix: run at least a light Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (per the library's technique catalog, the prescribed reach when "only one explanation is on the table," even a light pass covering just step 1 — is there a real alternative? — and step 5 — does the evidence actually discriminate?). Concretely: state the bust/complementary-role hypothesis alongside the star hypothesis and name one piece of evidence that would favor one over the other. The pattern here also matches Pherson's "satisficing" intuitive trap — stopping at the first good-enough (entirely positive) explanation — whose mitigation is the same ACH pass, not added vigilance.

2. **Certainty stated where a probability belongs** — Standard 2, Properly expresses and explains uncertainties
   > "Thornton will develop into a starter-caliber wing by year two. By years 3–5 he will be a 20+ ppg scorer in the NBA. By year 4, his motor and defensive IQ will make him a capable perimeter defensive anchor... this is a high-confidence franchise cornerstone selection."
   Why it matters: every clause is phrased as a flat future fact ("will"), not an estimate — there is no calibrated likelihood term anywhere in the product, and "high-confidence" is asserted with no evidence base named for what that confidence rests on. This is the library's "best-guess/uncertainty-drop" bias by name: an inherently uncertain, multi-year, multi-step outcome (rookie development curves are themselves uncertain, and this chains three of them: year 2, years 3–5, year 4) is being carried as if it were already 100% true.
   Fix: rewrite each projection on the ICD 203 seven-term ladder with a confidence statement held separate from it — e.g., "We assess it is likely (55–80%) that Thornton reaches a starter-caliber role by year two; confidence is moderate, resting on his shooting volume and one season of high-usage production we have not compared against a peer cohort." Remember the chain-probability rule: three sequential "will" claims compound, they don't just add up to one confident story.

3. **The comparable carries the argument with no stated basis** — Standard 1, Properly describes quality and credibility of sources, data, and methods
   > "COMPARABLE: Paul George (pre-injury trajectory)"
   Why it matters: a single elite-outcome comp is doing real evidentiary work here — it's the one thing in the product that concretizes what "franchise cornerstone" would look like — yet nothing characterizes it: no stated similarity criteria, no acknowledgment of the (low) base rate at which any given prospect comp actually hits, no mention of alternative comps considered and rejected. This is Pherson's "projecting past experience" trap by name ("this is like the last one, so…").
   Fix: name the basis for the comp (which measurables or role attributes it rests on), state at least one comp that argues the other way, and give the base rate for comps of this type panning out — per the library's mapping, this is mostly a Key Assumptions Check: what would have to be true about Thornton for the George comp to hold, and is that verified or assumed?

4. **Judgment folded into the data bullets with no signal phrase** — Standard 3, Properly distinguishes underlying information from analysts' assumptions and judgments
   > "Led team in charges taken (defensive intangibles)" ... "Dropped 28 points in the SEC championship game under pressure"
   Why it matters: "defensive intangibles" and "under pressure" are the analyst's interpretations, presented parenthetically inside what is labeled "SUPPORTING DATA" as though they were part of the same reported fact. A reader can't tell, from the text, where the box-score line ends and the inference (this reflects defensive value; this reflects clutch performance) begins — exactly the failure this standard exists to catch.
   Fix: split the bullet into the reported fact and a labeled judgment — "Led team in charges taken (reported). We judge this reflects above-average defensive activity (assumption: charges taken correlates with team defensive value, not verified here)."

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Run the light ACH pass before this goes out: write down the bust/role-player hypothesis next to the franchise-cornerstone hypothesis, and ask what single piece of evidence actually discriminates between them. Doing that one check would force "No significant negatives identified" to be either retracted or defended, and it would naturally pull the "will" projections down onto the calibrated probability ladder — both problems trace back to the same missing step.
