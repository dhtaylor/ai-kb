FORECAST: Pacific Northwest Wildfire Season Outlook

BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT: We assess with moderate confidence (60–75%) that the 2024 Pacific Northwest wildfire season will exceed the 10-year average burned area. The primary drivers are a below-normal spring snowpack and a projected blocking high-pressure ridge persisting into August. An alternative scenario — a wet June–July pattern analogous to 2011 — would suppress fire activity regardless of snowpack deficit; we assign this scenario roughly 25% probability based on current ensemble guidance.

KEY JUDGMENTS
1. Cascades snowpack measured 68% of April 1 median (NRCS SNOTEL network, observation-grade, 800+ sensor stations across the West). Snowpack deficit correlates with regional burned area at r ≈ 0.61 over 1990–2023; substantial interannual noise remains and the relationship is not deterministic.
2. Blocking ridge projections rest on NOAA operational seasonal outlooks (updated monthly, operationally validated) and University of Washington ensemble runs (research-grade, single institution — treat with commensurate caution; no independent corroboration cited here).
3. We do not assess a catastrophic single-event season comparable to the 2020 Labor Day fires as likely; that scenario requires specific ignition-timing and wind conditions not captured in seasonal outlooks and carries less than 15% probability.

CONFIDENCE CAVEATS: Seasonal fire forecast skill degrades substantially beyond six weeks. This assessment describes a probability distribution, not a point prediction. Reassess when June precipitation data are available.
