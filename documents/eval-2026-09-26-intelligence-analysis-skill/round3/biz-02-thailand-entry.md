## IA Review — Recommendation on APAC Expansion — Thailand Entry Decision

**Reviewed at:** Medium level — this is a memo recommending a capital decision to management; a memo is a listed Medium-level form regardless of the dollar amount at stake.
**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "We judge, with moderate confidence, that Thailand is the right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry."

### Strengths
- The bottom line leads the document, correctly positioned and labeled, not buried after background — satisfies the standard for clear, logical argumentation ("Is there a clear main message (BLUF) up front...?"). Quote: "BLUF: We judge, with moderate confidence, that Thailand is the right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry."
- Confidence is stated as a separate axis from the judgment and tied explicitly to the evidence base, naming the weak link rather than hiding it — satisfies the standard for properly expressing uncertainty. Quote: "Confidence is moderate: the Indonesia analogy is directionally sound but subject to Thai-specific channel dynamics we have not yet fully mapped."
- A genuine competing option is weighed with real reasoning, not a token mention — satisfies the standard for incorporating analysis of alternatives. Quote: "The strongest alternative is a Philippines-first entry, which offers a larger English-speaking talent pool and lower support-delivery cost."

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **The figure that rejects the strongest alternative is a small, single-source estimate treated as precise** — Standard: Properly expresses and explains uncertainties (also: the bias *Oversensitivity to consistency / law of small numbers*, per the library's cognitive-bias catalog)
   > "Philippine enterprise buying cycles run approximately 20% longer per our regional partner survey (Q4 2024, n=12 resellers), which delays breakeven by roughly one quarter and reduces NPV at our 12% hurdle rate."
   Why it matters: this single figure — from 12 resellers, one survey, one origin, not the enterprise buyers themselves — is the entire basis for demoting the strongest alternative to second-best, yet it is carried straight into a precise NPV/breakeven conclusion with no confidence caveat. The library's bias catalog names this pattern directly: a consistent-looking result from a small or correlated sample "feels strong" and gets treated as a stable effect rather than being checked against expected variation. If the true gap is smaller than ~20%, the case against Philippines weakens materially.
   Fix: Run the Quality-of-Information Check on this figure specifically — note that it is a single, non-independent source (resellers, not buyers) — and restate the effect as a range with explicit low confidence (e.g., "a small, single-source survey suggests longer Philippine cycles; we're more confident in the direction than the ~20%/one-quarter magnitude") rather than feeding a point estimate straight into the NPV rejection.

2. **Vietnam's regulatory disadvantage is asserted with no source at all** — Standard: Properly describes quality and credibility of sources, data, and methods
   > "its lower regulatory complexity relative to Vietnam make it the leading candidate"
   Why it matters: this is one of only two reasons given in the ASSESSMENT for ranking Thailand above the other reviewed markets, and unlike every other substantive claim in the memo (IDC report, customer interviews, cohort data, partner survey) it names no source, data point, or method — a bare comparative assertion doing real work in the leading judgment.
   Fix: Name the specific regulatory factors being compared (e.g., foreign-ownership caps, data-localization rules, licensing timeline) and attach what they're drawn from, or — per the Quality-of-Information Check — drop the claim from the reasoning chain until it can be sourced.

Overall: this is a well-built memo by the genre's own standards — BLUF correctly positioned, confidence and judgment kept separate, and a real alternative weighed rather than waved off. Its weak point is evidentiary discipline at the edges: one rejection of a competitor market leans on a thin sample dressed in precise-sounding numbers, and one comparative claim carries no source at all. Neither undermines the overall direction, but both are load-bearing enough in the ASSESSMENT to warrant tightening before this goes to the capital committee.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Run the Quality-of-Information Check on the Philippines buying-cycle figure (n=12, single source) before it anchors an NPV-based rejection — restate it as a range with explicit low confidence. It's the single most conclusion-threatening number in the memo because it's the one doing the work of ruling out the strongest alternative.
