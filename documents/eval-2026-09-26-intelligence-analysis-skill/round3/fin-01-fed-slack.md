## IA Review — fin-01-fed-slack (portfolio-risk Slack post)

**Reviewed at:** Light level — a single Slack channel message, not a memo or formal call; per the
skill's calibration, a chat message's form sets its level regardless of the dollar size of the trade
behind it.
**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "Tech multiples will compress and small-caps will
underperform large-caps through year-end."

### Strengths
- The action is concrete and unambiguous, not a vague gesture at "de-risking" — "Rotating: +5% TLT,
  −10% QQQ" gives the reader an exact, checkable position. This satisfies ICD 203 ATS #6 (uses clear
  and logical argumentation / a clear main message), which is one of the three standards that apply at
  this level.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)
1. **A single Fed remark is rounded up to a certain, multi-month market call** — ICD 203 ATS #8/#2
   ("Makes accurate judgments and assessments" / "Properly expresses and explains uncertainties") —
   the pair the library groups together as the Light-level check for overclaiming.
   > "Tech multiples will compress and small-caps will underperform large-caps through year-end."
   Why it matters: the entire rotation (+5% TLT, −10% QQQ) rests on this one sentence, and it states a
   multi-month, cross-asset outcome as flat fact ("will... will...") from one input — Powell holding
   the line on cuts — with no probability term and no confidence level attached. This is the
   Heuer bias catalog's **best-guess / uncertainty-drop** trap ("treating a 75%-likely input as 100%
   true"; prescribed mitigation: "carry the uncertainty through... rather than rounding to certain").
   Fix: state it as a calibrated estimate instead of a prediction — e.g., "Likely (55–80%) that tech
   multiples compress and small-caps underperform through year-end on this hold; confidence moderate —
   this is one data point on rate path, not a read on earnings or breadth." Adding one ICD 203
   probability term and a confidence line turns the overclaim into a properly hedged judgment; nothing
   else about the message needs to change.

### Scorecard (only the applicable standards)
| Standard | Rating | Anchor |
|----------|--------|--------|
| ATS #6 — Uses clear and logical argumentation | Good | "Rotating: +5% TLT, −10% QQQ" — a plain, checkable action |
| ATS #3 — Distinguishes information from assumptions/judgments | Fair | "Powell just confirmed no cuts before December" (information) runs straight into "Tech multiples will compress..." (judgment) with no signal phrase ("we assess," "we judge") marking the shift |
| ATS #8/#2 — Accurate, calibrated judgment (not overclaimed) | Poor | "will compress" / "will underperform" — no probability term, no confidence level |

Standard #4 (analysis of alternatives) does not apply at this level, per the library's own
calibration table, so no finding is raised for the absence of a competing scenario (e.g., that a
single hawkish hold has limited or already-priced-in effect on relative sector performance).

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Replace "will compress... will underperform" with a calibrated likelihood term and a stated
confidence level, tied explicitly to the one data point it rests on.
