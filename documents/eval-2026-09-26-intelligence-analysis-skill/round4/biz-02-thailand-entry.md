## IA Review — Recommendation on APAC Expansion — Thailand Entry Decision

**Reviewed at:** Medium level — it is a decision memo (a named Medium-level form) addressed to a
decision-maker being asked to approve a $1.2M budget; stakes don't promote a memo's form to Full.

**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "We judge, with moderate confidence, that Thailand is the
right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry. The risk-adjusted opportunity outweighs the alternatives
reviewed; however, if partner-channel depth proves shallower than scoped, the bear case favors
deferral to FY2027."

### Strengths
- The bottom line opens the memo, carries a calibrated confidence term, and names the condition that
  would flip it — satisfies Standard #6 (clear argumentation, main message up front): "We judge, with
  moderate confidence, that Thailand is the right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry... if
  partner-channel depth proves shallower than scoped, the bear case favors deferral to FY2027."
- The memo's own key proxy is flagged as an assumption rather than presented as fact — satisfies
  Standard #3 (information vs. assumption vs. judgment): "Confidence is moderate: the Indonesia
  analogy is directionally sound but subject to Thai-specific channel dynamics we have not yet fully
  mapped."
- The recommendation ends in a monitorable, falsifiable commitment rather than an open-ended approval —
  satisfies Standard #5 (customer relevance / so-what): "Formal go/no-go review at month six against
  two leading indicators: pipeline coverage ratio ≥3× and partner NPS ≥40."

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **Uncited comparative claim carries the case against the named alternative** — Standard #1
   (properly describes quality and credibility of sources, data, and methods)
   > "its lower regulatory complexity relative to Vietnam make it the leading candidate"
   Why it matters: this claim, alongside the (explicitly hedged) CAC-to-LTV proxy, is one of only two
   reasons given for favoring Thailand over Vietnam specifically — and unlike every other comparative
   figure in the memo (market size, WTP, buying-cycle length), it carries no source, method, or basis
   at all. If it's wrong or overstated, the memo loses half its stated case against Vietnam, which
   never otherwise gets the kind of alternative-weighing the memo gives Philippines.
   Fix: run a Quality-of-Information Check on this specific claim — name an actual source (e.g., a
   World Bank Doing Business ranking, or in-house legal/tax counsel's regulatory assessment) or, absent
   one, relabel it explicitly as an unverified assumption rather than letting it stand as settled fact
   next to the sourced figures around it.

2. **Small, single-origin sample carried into the favorable narrative without a confidence discount** —
   Standard #1, and the library's "oversensitivity to consistency / law of small numbers" bias
   (evidence-evaluation chapter)
   > "Our own customer-discovery interviews (n=18 Thai enterprise buyers, Q1 2025, conducted by our
   regional BD team) show willingness-to-pay at 85–90% of US rack rates and a preference for
   local-language support our Singapore team can already provide."
   Why it matters: n=18, gathered in one quarter by the single internal team that also owns the
   expansion case, is exactly the pattern the library names — a consistent story from a few correlated
   sources feels stronger than it is. The memo applies real rigor to discounting its Indonesia-proxy
   figure two paragraphs later but does not apply the same discount here, letting an internally
   sourced, small-n figure carry weight in the favorable case unqualified.
   Fix: per the library's prescribed mitigation, state this figure at reduced confidence given its
   small size and single/correlated origin, and seek one corroborating, independently sourced data
   point (a third-party WTP benchmark, or buyer interviews run outside the regional BD team) before it
   continues to carry weight in the recommendation.

### Scorecard (only the applicable standards)

| Standard | Rating | Anchor |
|----------|--------|--------|
| #1 Sourcing quality/credibility | Fair | "lower regulatory complexity relative to Vietnam" — asserted with no source, against an otherwise well-sourced memo |
| #2 Expresses uncertainty | Good | "Confidence is moderate: the Indonesia analogy is directionally sound but subject to Thai-specific channel dynamics we have not yet fully mapped" |
| #3 Information vs. assumption vs. judgment | Good | "We assess that Thailand's favorable CAC-to-LTV profile — estimated 2.8× based on our Indonesia cohort (nearest proxy...)" |
| #4 Analysis of alternatives | Good | Philippines weighed with a quantified trade-off: "delays breakeven by roughly one quarter and reduces NPV at our 12% hurdle rate" |
| #5 Customer relevance / so-what | Excellent | "Approve a Thailand pilot with a $1.2M budget cap... Formal go/no-go review at month six against two leading indicators" |
| #6 Clear, logical argumentation | Good | BLUF opens the memo and states the condition that would reverse it |
| #8 Accurate judgments, confidence aligned to evidence | Fair | Moderate confidence is well-calibrated overall, but the uncited Vietnam claim and the undiscounted small-sample WTP figure both overclaim certainty relative to their evidentiary basis |

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Source or drop the "lower regulatory complexity relative to Vietnam" claim — it's the single unsupported
assertion propping up half the case against the memo's nearest named alternative, in a memo that is
otherwise careful to cite and hedge everything else.
