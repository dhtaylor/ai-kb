## IA Review — Dengue Transmission Risk, Andean Region, Q3 2025

**Reviewed at:** Full level — an epidemiological forecast prepared by a WHO/PAHO regional surveillance unit for health-ministry decision-makers; "forecast" and "formal assessment" are named directly in the Full tier's list of forms, so stakes and audience don't need to promote it further.

**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "Dengue transmission across Colombia, Peru, and Ecuador will likely remain above the 10-year seasonal median through August 2025 (assessed probability: ~60–70%), driven by residual DENV-3 serotype circulation and above-average precipitation in lowland endemic zones."

### Strengths
- The bottom line leads the product, in its opening sentence, with the reasoning that supports it stated immediately alongside it — satisfies Standard #6 (clear and logical argumentation), including the position rule for where a judgment must sit.
- Uncertainty is calibrated and confidence is kept separate from likelihood, matching Standard #2: "assessed probability: ~60–70%" is a bounded range, and "Overall analytic confidence is moderate, reflecting El Niño–La Niña transition uncertainty" ties the confidence statement to a named evidentiary reason rather than a bare hedge.
- A genuine competing scenario is on the table with its own probability, not token acknowledgment — Standard #4: "Transmission returns to seasonal baseline by July if La Niña onset occurs earlier than the ECMWF June median projection (~30% probability)."

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **The revision looks anchored to the prior estimate rather than re-derived from the new evidence** — Standard #7 (explains change to or consistency of analytic judgments) and Standard #8 (accurate judgments); bias: **Anchoring** (Heuer, biases in estimating probability).
   > "This updates and revises upward our March 2025 assessment (50–60%), reflecting a confirmed serotype shift and April precipitation anomalies."
   Why it matters: two separately-cited pieces of new evidence (a confirmed serotype shift and a precipitation anomaly) produce a uniform +10-point shift on both ends of the range (50–60% → 60–70%), with no explanation of why that particular magnitude follows from those inputs. The library names exactly this pattern as the revision tell-tale for anchoring: "an updated estimate that barely moves from the prior one despite newly cited risks/evidence — name it as anchoring, not as a reasonable update." Two apparently material new inputs should be re-derived, not slid.
   Fix: Re-estimate from scratch, per the prescribed mitigation for anchoring — treat the serotype and precipitation evidence as inputs to a fresh probability judgment rather than an increment on March's number, and show the reasoning connecting each input to the size of the revision.

2. **The serotype-shift claim — one of the two named drivers of the bottom line — is never source-characterized** — Standard #1 (properly describes quality and credibility of sources, data, and methods).
   > "driven by residual DENV-3 serotype circulation... reflecting a confirmed serotype shift" — absent: no surveillance system, lab network, or reliability assessment is named for this claim anywhere in the product.
   Why it matters: the BASIS AND SOURCE RELIABILITY section characterizes the other two inputs in detail (case-report capture rate benchmarked at ~75% against 2019–2023 active surveillance, ECMWF skill score of 0.72), but is silent on where "confirmed serotype shift" comes from or how reliable that confirmation is — even though it is named first among the drivers of the core judgment.
   Fix: Run a Quality-of-Information Check on the serotype-shift claim — name the typing/surveillance source, its coverage, and its reliability, the same way the other two inputs already are.

3. **The "low-regret" framing rests on an unexamined logistics assumption** — Standard #1 and Standard #8 (accurate judgments, not overclaimed).
   > "Stocks pre-positioned under the baseline scenario remain available for 2026 at minimal carrying cost if the alternative materializes."
   Why it matters: this sentence is what makes the SO WHAT recommendation "low-regret," but it asserts shelf life and storage economics for vector-control supplies as settled fact, with no source and no hedge. If that premise is wrong — commodities with limited shelf life, or ministries with tight storage budgets — the recommendation's risk calculus changes.
   Fix: Key Assumptions Check on this specific premise — state what it depends on (shelf life of the specific commodities, storage cost, budget elasticity), and flag which of those are verified versus merely assumed.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Re-derive the 60–70% figure from the two newly cited inputs instead of incrementing March's range — show how the confirmed serotype shift and the precipitation anomaly each move the estimate, so the reader sees reasoning rather than a mechanical +10-point slide.
