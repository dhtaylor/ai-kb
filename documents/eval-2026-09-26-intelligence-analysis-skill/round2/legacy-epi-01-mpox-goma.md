## IA Review — Mpox Clade Ib Cluster — Goma District (memo, 14 March 2025)

**Reviewed at:** Full level — a formal epi-cell memo to a Regional Health Director recommending against expanding the response footprint for an active clade Ib mpox cluster; consequential, externally read, and directly actionable.

**Analytic line (BLUF as written):** "We therefore assess that sustained community transmission beyond the index market cluster is unlikely" — paired with the recommendation "Maintain current surveillance posture. No expansion of the response footprint is warranted at this time." (Both appear after the case-count lead, not in the opening sentences.)

### Strengths
- **Exact tracing numbers, not a rounded percentage.** "Of 337 identified contacts, 289 have been reached" lets the reader do the math and see the real gap (48 untraced) rather than hiding it behind "68%." Good practice under ATS #1.
- **Judgment clearly signaled, not blended with fact.** "No secondary cases have been identified among reached contacts. We therefore assess..." uses a clean signal phrase to separate the observation from the inference — exactly what ATS #3 asks for.
- **Etiologic claim is properly sourced.** "Laboratory sequencing confirms clade Ib" cites a specific method rather than an unattributed assertion.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **Buried BLUF** — Standard #6 (clear and logical argumentation)
   > "Over the past three weeks, 47 confirmed and 12 probable mpox clade Ib cases have been reported across Goma district."
   Why it matters: this is the opening sentence, and it's a case count, not the judgment. The actual bottom line — transmission looks contained, no response expansion needed — doesn't appear until paragraph 2 (assessment) and paragraph 4 (recommendation). A reader acting on the first sentence alone gets prevalence data, not the decision. This is the disguised case of a memo that leads with prevalence data before the bottom-line assessment.
   Fix: Open with the judgment and recommendation in the first two sentences — "We assess sustained community transmission beyond the Nyiragongo market cluster is unlikely; no expansion of the response footprint is recommended at this time." — then move the case counts into the supporting paragraph.

2. **Unexamined premise behind the core judgment** — Standard #4 (analysis of alternatives) / Key Assumptions Check
   > "No secondary cases have been identified among reached contacts. We therefore assess that sustained community transmission beyond the index market cluster is unlikely."
   Why it matters: this is the single inference the whole recommendation rests on, and it generalizes from the 68% of contacts actually reached (289/337) to a community-wide claim. Nothing addresses what the 48 unreached contacts might carry, whether transmission could be running through a chain outside the identified network (mpox spreads via close/sexual contact that a market-linked contact list won't necessarily capture), or whether the reached contacts have completed a full incubation-period observation window rather than simply not having symptoms yet. No competing hypothesis to "contained to the market cluster" is ever put on the table.
   Fix: Run a Key Assumptions Check on this judgment and state the premises explicitly: (a) no undetected chain exists among the 48 untraced contacts; (b) reached contacts have passed the observation window; (c) transmission is fully captured by contacts linked to the index market. Flag which are unverified and let that drive the confidence statement (see #3).

3. **Confidence not stated alongside the likelihood term** — Standard #2 (expresses uncertainty)
   > "We therefore assess that sustained community transmission beyond the index market cluster is unlikely."
   Why it matters: "unlikely" is a correctly-used calibrated term, but likelihood and confidence are two different axes, and only one is given here. The evidence behind the call is incomplete — 68% tracing completion, no case-definition or testing-capacity detail, a three-week window — so a reader can't tell if this is a solid call or a fragile one wearing calibrated language.
   Fix: State confidence separately, e.g. "confidence is moderate, resting on 68% contact-tracing completion; the untraced 32% and any transmission pathway outside the identified contact network are the main sources of residual uncertainty."

4. **Unquantified linkage claim** — Standard #1 (source/data quality)
   > "Case interviews indicate a majority had contact with a market in Nyiragongo commune."
   Why it matters: "a majority" of 59 cases is never given a number, and the interviews themselves aren't characterized (how many of the 59 cases were actually interviewed, recall reliability). This vague claim is what frames the whole cluster as attributable to one contained source — the premise the rest of the memo is built on.
   Fix: Replace with the actual figures, e.g. "38 of 47 confirmed cases (81%), based on interviews completed with 52 of 59 total cases, reported market contact."

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Rewrite the lead so the judgment and recommendation come first, and fold the tracing gap directly into that sentence as a confidence caveat — e.g., "We assess (moderate confidence) that sustained community transmission is unlikely; the main uncertainty is the 48 contacts (32%) not yet reached." That single move fixes the buried BLUF and forces the load-bearing assumption into the open where the Regional Health Director can weigh it, rather than leaving "unlikely" to carry more certainty than the evidence supports.
