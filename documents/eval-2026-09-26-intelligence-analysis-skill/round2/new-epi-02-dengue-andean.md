## IA Review — Dengue Transmission Risk, Andean Region, Q3 2025 (WHO/PAHO Regional Surveillance Unit, 01 June 2025)

**Reviewed at:** Full level — a formal WHO/PAHO regional forecast addressed to health ministries, driving a pre-positioning/procurement decision; this is a listed Full-level form (forecast/formal assessment), so all nine ICD 203 standards apply, with #4 and #7 explicitly evaluated (prior March 2025 coverage exists, so #7 is in scope, not exempt).

**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "Dengue transmission across Colombia, Peru, and Ecuador will likely remain above the 10-year seasonal median through August 2025 (assessed probability: ~60–70%), driven by residual DENV-3 serotype circulation and above-average precipitation in lowland endemic zones."

### Strengths
- **BLUF correctly positioned, not just present** — the main judgment is the first sentence, before any background, with a calibrated range attached ("~60–70%") that sits cleanly inside a single rung of the estimative-probability ladder ("likely," 55–80%) rather than mixing terms — satisfies Standard #6 (clear and logical argumentation).
- **Confidence kept separate from likelihood** — "Overall analytic confidence is moderate, reflecting El Niño–La Niña transition uncertainty" states the evidence-base quality as its own axis rather than folding it into the probability term, and ties it to a named source of uncertainty — satisfies Standard #2.
- **A real alternative with a discriminator, not a token one** — "Transmission returns to seasonal baseline by July if La Niña onset occurs earlier than the ECMWF June median projection (~30% probability)" names an observable event that would actually distinguish the two scenarios, rather than just asserting an alternative exists — satisfies Standard #4.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **The revision's central driver is never sourced** — Standard #1: Properly describes quality and credibility of sources, data, and methods
   > "reflecting a confirmed serotype shift and April precipitation anomalies"
   Why it matters: the BASIS section characterizes exactly two sources — PAHO/MoH case reports and ECMWF precipitation forecasts — but "residual DENV-3 serotype circulation" / "a confirmed serotype shift" is the driver named first in the bottom line and is what moves the estimate off the March baseline. Serotype data normally comes from a distinct virological/laboratory surveillance stream, not case-count reports, and no source, date, or corroboration is given for it anywhere in the product. If this claim doesn't hold up, the entire upward revision loses its footing.
   Fix: Run a Quality-of-Information Check on this specific claim before anything else — name the actual surveillance product (e.g., a PAHO arbovirus/serotype bulletin or national reference-lab typing data), its date, and whether more than one lab corroborates it. Until then, flag it explicitly as an unattributed input rather than a "confirmed" fact.

2. **The size of the revision doesn't match the weight of the new evidence — a tell-tale anchoring pattern** — Standard #7: Explains change to or consistency of analytic judgments
   > "This updates and revises upward our March 2025 assessment (50–60%), reflecting a confirmed serotype shift and April precipitation anomalies."
   Why it matters: the product does explain that this is a revision (correctly satisfying the existence check), but a confirmed serotype shift in dengue epidemiology is historically associated with substantial jumps in outbreak risk (new-serotype introductions typically drive the largest epidemic years), yet the estimate moved only ten points. The library names this exact shape — "an updated estimate that barely moves from the prior one despite newly cited risks/evidence" — as the tell-tale of **Anchoring**, not a reasoned update.
   Fix: Re-estimate from scratch rather than incrementing off the March number. Independently size what the serotype shift alone would imply for outbreak probability (e.g., against the region's own history of serotype-shift years) and what the precipitation anomaly alone implies, then show the combined range is the product of that reasoning — not a nudge on the prior figure.

3. **Precise-looking source metrics without an identifiable publication behind them** — Standard #1: Properly describes quality and credibility of sources, data, and methods
   > "estimated to capture ~75% of true incidence based on 2019–2023 active surveillance benchmarks" and "verified at a 0.72 skill score for the Andean region"
   Why it matters: these read as well-characterized sourcing because they carry numbers and a date range, but a "benchmark" and a "skill score" are method labels, not a cited document or dataset — no report, bulletin, or verification study is named. A number wearing the costume of sourcing is still uncited, and both figures materially support the "moderate reliability" and "moderate confidence" claims that follow.
   Fix: Replace the benchmark labels with the actual references — the specific PAHO/MoH surveillance-completeness study behind the 75% figure and the specific ECMWF verification bulletin behind the 0.72 skill score, each dated.

4. **A cost claim asserted as fact, not as a labeled judgment** — Standard #3: Properly distinguishes underlying information from analysts' assumptions and judgments
   > "Stocks pre-positioned under the baseline scenario remain available for 2026 at minimal carrying cost if the alternative materializes."
   Why it matters: this sentence carries the entire "low-regret" framing in the SO WHAT section, but it's stated flatly, with no signal phrase ("we judge," "we assess") and no basis (whose stock, what cold-chain/storage capacity, what actual carrying cost was estimated). It reads as established fact when it is at best an analyst assumption about logistics the product hasn't examined.
   Fix: Either cut the claim or convert it explicitly: "We judge carrying cost is minimal (assumption: current storage capacity absorbs the surplus without incremental spend — not independently verified)."

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Source the serotype-shift claim (finding 1) before anything else — it's the single fact the entire upward revision rests on, and right now it has no more standing in the text than an assertion.
