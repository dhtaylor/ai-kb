## IA Review — Dengue Transmission Risk, Andean Region, Q3 2025 (WHO/PAHO Regional Surveillance Unit, 01 June 2025)

**Reviewed at:** Full level — a formal epidemiological forecast from a regional surveillance unit, addressed to health ministries, driving a resourcing/procurement decision (vector control pre-positioning).

**Analytic line (BLUF as written):** "Dengue transmission across Colombia, Peru, and Ecuador will likely remain above the 10-year seasonal median through August 2025 (assessed probability: ~60–70%), driven by residual DENV-3 serotype circulation and above-average precipitation in lowland endemic zones."

### Strengths
- **BLUF is immediate and quantified (ATS #6).** The judgment leads the product in the first sentence under "BOTTOM LINE," with a calibrated probability range attached — no setup, chronology, or background precedes it.
- **Likelihood and confidence are stated separately and calibrated (ATS #2).** > "assessed probability: ~60–70%... Overall analytic confidence is moderate, reflecting El Niño–La Niña transition uncertainty." The two are not conflated, and confidence is tied to a named source of uncertainty rather than asserted.
- **A genuine competing scenario is quantified and tied to the decision (ATS #4 / #5).** > "Transmission returns to seasonal baseline by July if La Niña onset occurs earlier than the ECMWF June median projection (~30% probability)... low-regret under either scenario." The alternative isn't a token mention — it has its own probability and the recommendation is explicitly framed to survive it.
- **The analytic line is stated (ATS #7).** > "This updates and revises upward our March 2025 assessment (50–60%), reflecting a confirmed serotype shift and April precipitation anomalies" — tells the reader this is a revision and why, rather than leaving them to guess whether anything changed.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)
1. **Uncited case-capture benchmark propping up the primary source's reliability rating** — Standard #1 (source quality and credibility)
   > "assessed as moderately reliable, estimated to capture ~75% of true incidence based on 2019–2023 active surveillance benchmarks"
   Why it matters: this figure carries the reliability judgment for the product's primary evidence base (PAHO/MoH case reports), which in turn underwrites the "moderate confidence" behind the headline probability. But "2019–2023 active surveillance benchmarks" names a date range and a methodology label, not an identifiable publication, dataset, or study — it's a number dressed as sourcing, not sourcing. Contrast with the ECMWF input two sentences later, which *is* properly characterized ("verified at a 0.72 skill score for the Andean region"). The asymmetry means the reader can't actually tell how solid the case-count leg of the forecast is.
   Fix: cite the specific completeness/underreporting study or surveillance-evaluation report the 75% figure comes from. If no such publication exists and the number is an internal analytic estimate, say so explicitly ("analyst estimate, not independently validated") and reflect that in the confidence language rather than presenting it alongside a validated, skill-scored dataset as if the two were equally grounded.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Attribute the ~75% case-capture estimate to its actual source (or flag it as an unvalidated internal estimate). It's the one number in the product that's dressed up as sourced but isn't, and it's load-bearing for how much the reader should trust the case-report leg of the forecast.
