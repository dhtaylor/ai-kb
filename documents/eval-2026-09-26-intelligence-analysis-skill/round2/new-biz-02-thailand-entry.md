## IA Review — Recommendation on APAC Expansion — Thailand Entry Decision

**Reviewed at:** Medium level — a decision memo to management recommending a budget commitment; its form ("a memo, a spike/design recommendation") sets the level regardless of the dollar stakes.
**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "We judge, with moderate confidence, that Thailand is the right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry."

### Strengths
- The bottom line leads the product, not the background — "BLUF: We judge, with moderate confidence, that Thailand is the right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry" appears before any market data or history, satisfying the position rule for Standard #6 (clear and logical argumentation) rather than arriving after throat-clearing context.
- Confidence is stated as its own axis and tied explicitly to the evidence base rather than asserted alone: "Confidence is moderate: the Indonesia analogy is directionally sound but subject to Thai-specific channel dynamics we have not yet fully mapped" — this is Standard #2 done correctly, naming the weak link instead of burying it.
- The recommendation sets observation milestones before committing further capital — "Formal go/no-go review at month six against two leading indicators: pipeline coverage ratio ≥3× and partner NPS ≥40" — which is the Indicators/Signposts technique already applied, giving Standard #5 (customer relevance) a monitorable, non-rhetorical commitment rather than a vague promise to "keep watching."

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)

1. **Alternative eliminated on a different, thinner yardstick than the one used to rank Thailand** — Standard #4, Incorporates analysis of alternatives
   > "The strongest alternative is a Philippines-first entry, which offers a larger English-speaking talent pool and lower support-delivery cost... Philippine enterprise buying cycles run approximately 20% longer per our regional partner survey (Q4 2024, n=12 resellers), which delays breakeven by roughly one quarter and reduces NPV at our 12% hurdle rate."
   Why it matters: Thailand was ranked against Malaysia and Vietnam using a consistent, sourced metric (IDC market size and YoY growth). The Philippines — the one alternative actually weighed — is never given that same figure; it is dismissed on a single, different metric (buying-cycle length) from a thin source. A light Analysis of Competing Hypotheses pass asks whether the evidence actually discriminates between the two candidates; here it's unclear whether it does, because the two options were never measured on the same scale. The "second-best" call may not survive an apples-to-apples comparison.
   Fix: Run the light ACH check named in the structured-analytic-techniques library (step 5: does any evidence actually discriminate, or is it consistent with both?) — put a Philippines market-size/growth figure next to Thailand's $620M/18% before letting buying-cycle timing carry the elimination.

2. **A precise financial conclusion rests on an uncharacterized, small-n source** — Standard #1, Properly describes quality and credibility of sources
   > "Philippine enterprise buying cycles run approximately 20% longer per our regional partner survey (Q4 2024, n=12 resellers), which delays breakeven by roughly one quarter and reduces NPV at our 12% hurdle rate."
   Why it matters: n=12 is cited (which is more than a bare claim) but never characterized for reliability, independence, or corroboration — the library names this exact pattern as the "oversensitivity to consistency / law of small numbers" bias: a consistent-sounding story from a few, possibly correlated respondents (resellers, likely sharing the same regional vantage point) feels stronger than it is, and its prescribed mitigation is to carry that uncertainty forward rather than convert it into a specific "one quarter" delay and hurdle-rate effect.
   Fix: Run the Quality-of-Information Check on this source before it drives a dollarized NPV claim — state it as a range ("a modest but uncertain delay, on a 12-respondent sample") rather than "roughly one quarter," or note the sample size as a caveat the way the Indonesia-cohort estimate already does elsewhere in the memo.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Before this goes to management, put Philippines on the same footing as Thailand, Malaysia, and Vietnam — a market-size-and-growth figure, not just a buying-cycle delta from twelve resellers — so the "second-best" call is a real comparison rather than one metric standing in for the whole case.
