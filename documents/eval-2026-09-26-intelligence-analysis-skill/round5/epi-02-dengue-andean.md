## IA Review — Epidemiological Forecast: Dengue Transmission Risk, Andean Region, Q3 2025

**Reviewed at:** Full level. It is a standalone forecast written to be relied on by health ministries, with a stated bottom line, stated confidence, laid-out basis and a decision recommendation.
**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "Dengue transmission across Colombia, Peru, and Ecuador will likely remain above the 10-year seasonal median through August 2025 (assessed probability: ~60–70%), driven by residual DENV-3 serotype circulation and above-average precipitation in lowland endemic zones."

### Strengths
- The main message is first and unambiguous, and likelihood and confidence are kept on separate axes. The term "likely" sits in the 55–80% row and matches "~60–70%". Confidence is stated in a different sentence ("Overall analytic confidence is moderate, reflecting El Niño–La Niña transition uncertainty") and its cause is named. This meets standard 6 (main message up front) and standard 2 (uncertainty), and avoids the combined-sentence prohibition.
- The change from prior analysis is explicit and explained: "This updates and revises upward our March 2025 assessment (50–60%), reflecting a confirmed serotype shift and April precipitation anomalies." This meets standard 7.
- Customer relevance is concrete: "Health ministries should pre-position vector control supplies no later than mid-July... low-regret under either scenario." The alternative scenario's cost is also addressed ("remain available for 2026 at minimal carrying cost"). This meets standard 5.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)
1. **The upward revision rests on drivers with no identified source, and one source figure has no named basis.** Standard 1: "Properly describes quality and credibility of underlying sources, data, and methodologies". Standard 3: distinguish "underlying intelligence information" from "assumptions or judgments".
   > "reflecting a confirmed serotype shift and April precipitation anomalies" and "estimated to capture ~75% of true incidence based on 2019–2023 active surveillance benchmarks"
   Why it matters: the Basis section names only case reports and ECMWF seasonal *forecasts*. Neither is identified as the source of the "confirmed" serotype shift or of the April *observed* anomalies. Those two items are the stated reason the probability moved from 50–60% to 60–70%.
   The "~75%" capture rate carries a date range and a method label ("benchmarks") but names no study or dataset. It is a figure dressed as sourcing, and it sets how far case counts understate incidence relative to the median. The "0.72 skill score" also names neither the metric nor who verified it.
   "driven by" presents a causal judgment as established fact, mixed in with the observations.
   Fix: add a source summary statement that names (a) the serotyping source and date for DENV-3, (b) the precipitation dataset for April, and (c) the publication behind the 75% figure. Label the causal link as a judgment, for example "We judge the above-median outlook is driven by...". The library has no further remedy for this beyond the standards' wording.
2. **The alternative scenario's probability is inconsistent with its own trigger.** Standard 6: "internally consistent".
   > "if La Niña onset occurs earlier than the ECMWF June median projection (~30% probability)"
   Why it matters: by definition, an onset earlier than a median projection has about a 50% chance, not 30%. If 50% is closer to right, a 60–70% headline for the main case is hard to sustain. If 30% reflects some other reasoning, that reasoning is hidden. Either way the key probabilities do not reconcile.
   Fix: state how 30% was derived from the ECMWF spread. For example: "ECMWF puts onset before July at ~30%; we therefore give the baseline-return scenario ~30% and the above-median scenario ~60–70%." Or revise one of the two numbers.
3. **The alternatives analysis covers one driver only.** Standard 4: "identify and assess plausible alternative hypotheses".
   > "ALTERNATIVE SCENARIO: Transmission returns to seasonal baseline by July if La Niña onset occurs earlier..."
   Why it matters: it tests only the precipitation driver. It does not test the other stated driver, DENV-3 circulation, or the possibility that the above-median reading is a reporting artifact. That matters because the product itself says reporting captures only about 75% and the serotype driver is newly "confirmed". The recommendation survives anyway, because the action is low-regret.
   Fix: add one sentence per driver: "If DENV-3 circulation fades or case reporting rises, the expected effect is X; the indicator to watch is Y." Also define the metric behind "above the 10-year seasonal median" (cases or incidence, aggregate or per country), so the forecast can be scored later.

Overall read: this is a well-built forecast. The structure, calibration language and decision framing are strong. The weaknesses are about traceability and consistency rather than structure. The sourcing of the two drivers and the 30% figure are the places where a reader could not check the argument.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Source and label the two drivers behind the upward revision (the serotype confirmation and the April anomalies). Then reconcile the 30% alternative-scenario probability with the "earlier than median" trigger. The revised headline probability depends on both.
