## IA Review: Recommendation on APAC Expansion, Thailand Entry Decision

**Reviewed at:** Medium level. It is a recommendation memo to a decision-maker, and memos are Medium on this skill's ladder whatever the size of the spend. It is not a standalone formal assessment.
**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "We judge, with moderate confidence, that Thailand is the right first APAC market for a FY2026 entry. The risk-adjusted opportunity outweighs the alternatives reviewed; however, if partner-channel depth proves shallower than scoped, the bear case favors deferral to FY2027."

### Strengths
- **Bottom line is first, with confidence and a named defeater.** The BLUF puts the judgment, "moderate confidence", and the condition that flips it ("if partner-channel depth proves shallower than scoped") in the opening sentences. This meets the ICD 203 standard 6 position requirement ("a clear main analytic message up front") and standard 2, where likelihood and confidence are expressed.
- **The recommendation is tied to falsifiable checks.** "Formal go/no-go review at month six against two leading indicators: pipeline coverage ratio ≥3× and partner NPS ≥40. If either threshold is missed, management will present a revised entry or deferral plan." This turns the stated uncertainty into something observable.
- **Information, assumption and judgment are mostly kept apart.** The proxy status of the Indonesia number is labelled ("nearest proxy, n=200 active accounts, 24-month vintage"). Confidence is stated separately ("Confidence is moderate: the Indonesia analogy is directionally sound but subject to Thai-specific channel dynamics we have not yet fully mapped"). This supports standard 3.

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)
1. **The "Thailand beats Philippines" comparison is not like-for-like.** Standard 4, "Incorporates analysis of alternatives": products "should identify and assess plausible alternative hypotheses."
   > "Philippine enterprise buying cycles run approximately 20% longer per our regional partner survey (Q4 2024, n=12 resellers), which delays breakeven by roughly one quarter and reduces NPV at our 12% hurdle rate."
   > "its lower regulatory complexity relative to Vietnam"

   Why it matters: the conclusion rests on Thailand outranking the alternatives, but the two markets are measured on different things.
   - Thailand gets a CAC-to-LTV figure (2.8×). The Philippines gets a cycle-length delta.
   - The memo gives no Thai buying-cycle length for the 20% to be measured against.
   - No Thai NPV is shown, although the Philippines is said to "reduce NPV".
   - The one-quarter breakeven delay is asserted, not derived.
   - The Vietnam regulatory claim has no source.
   - Malaysia ($510M, 14% growth) appears in the background but is never assessed.

   Fix: put Thailand, the Philippines and Vietnam in one table on the same three or four measures (buying cycle, CAC:LTV or payback, NPV at 12%, regulatory burden). Mark each cell as sourced, estimated or unknown. Then run a light pass of Heuer's ACH step 3 diagnosticity on that matrix: "evidence 'consistent with only one hypothesis' is diagnostic." Market-size growth rates that fit all three markets are not diagnostic and should not carry the argument. State which evidence actually separates Thailand from the Philippines.

2. **The Indonesia-to-Thailand proxy is the load-bearing assumption and its validity is only asserted.** Standard 3, "Properly distinguishes between underlying intelligence information and analysts' assumptions and judgments."
   > "estimated 2.8× based on our Indonesia cohort (nearest proxy…)… the Indonesia analogy is directionally sound"

   Why it matters: the memo flags the gap (Thai channel dynamics are unmapped), so the caveat is not missing. "Directionally sound" is a bare assertion, though. Nothing says why Indonesia resembles Thailand, or what would show it does not. That leaves the "risk-adjusted" claim in the BLUF resting on an unexamined premise.
   Fix: run a **Key Assumptions Check**. Write down the analytic line and list the premises that must be true for 2.8× to transfer, such as channel mix, deal size and support cost. Challenge each one, and say what finding would force a rethink. Add the Indonesia-validity premise to the month-six indicators, since only partner NPS and pipeline coverage are gated now.

3. **The sole buyer-side evidence is self-collected, and its limits are not characterized.** Standard 1, "Properly describes quality and credibility of underlying sources, data, and methodologies."
   > "customer-discovery interviews (n=18 Thai enterprise buyers, Q1 2025, conducted by our regional BD team) show willingness-to-pay at 85–90% of US rack rates"

   Why it matters: this is the only demand-side support specific to Thailand. A sales-incentivized team ran the interviews, the figure is stated willingness-to-pay rather than observed purchase, and n=18 is small. None of these limits is noted, so the pricing premise is stronger on the page than its basis allows.
   Fix: run a **Quality of Information Check** on this item. Add a source line covering how the 18 buyers were selected, who conducted the interviews, and that the data is stated rather than revealed price. Say how pricing at 85–90% of US rates feeds the 2.8× or the NPV, and what happens to the case at a lower price point.

### Scorecard (applicable standards only)
| Standard | Rating | Anchor |
|----------|--------|--------|
| 1. Describes quality and credibility of sources | Fair | Named reports and survey dates are given, but "conducted by our regional BD team" has no caveats, and the Vietnam regulatory claim has no source |
| 2. Expresses and explains uncertainties | Good | "moderate confidence"; "directionally sound but subject to Thai-specific channel dynamics we have not yet fully mapped"; month-six gate |
| 3. Distinguishes information from assumptions and judgments | Fair | The proxy is labelled, but its validity is asserted ("directionally sound") and not examined |
| 4. Analysis of alternatives | Fair | A real alternative is named, but the metrics differ (cycle delta against CAC:LTV), and Malaysia and Vietnam are left unassessed |
| 5. Customer relevance and implications | Good | Budget cap, indicators with thresholds, and a fallback ("revised entry or deferral plan") |
| 6. Clear and logical argumentation | Good | The main message is up front, and the bear case acknowledges contrary information |
| 7. Change or consistency of judgments | N/A | There is no prior position to compare against |
| 8. Accurate judgments | Good | A commitment is made with a stated confidence, and the memo does not hedge it away |
| 9. Visual information | N/A | Not applicable at this level |

Overall read: this is a well-structured, honest recommendation. The weakness is that the decisive comparison is argued from unlike evidence. The product states its confidence honestly, and the pilot design is a sensible hedge, which lowers the cost of getting the market choice wrong.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Rebuild the alternatives paragraph as a side-by-side comparison of Thailand, the Philippines and Vietnam on identical measures. Show for each measure which cells are sourced and which are estimated, and say what separates the markets. This is the cheapest fix and it supports the claim the BLUF rests on.
