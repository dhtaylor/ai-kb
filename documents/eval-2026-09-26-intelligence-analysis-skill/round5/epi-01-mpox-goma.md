## IA Review — Mpox Clade Ib Cluster, Goma District (Epidemiological Analysis Cell memo, 14 March 2025)

**Reviewed at:** Medium level. It is a one-page memo to a single decision-maker (the Regional Health Director) that recommends a response posture. A memo is a Medium form, and the stakes do not promote it to Full.
**Analytic line (bottom line as written):** "We therefore assess that sustained community transmission beyond the index market cluster is unlikely." The recommendation is "Maintain current surveillance posture. No expansion of the response footprint is warranted at this time."

### Strengths
- The evidence base is named and the sequencing result is stated plainly. This meets standard 1 (sources, data and methodologies), though only partly. Quote: "Case interviews indicate a majority had contact with a market in Nyiragongo commune. Laboratory sequencing confirms clade Ib."
- The memo keeps the reported counts separate from its own inference. "We therefore assess" marks the point where the judgment starts. This partly meets standard 3 (information vs. assumptions and judgments).
- It flags the health-worker risk itself ("Risk to health workers remains a concern"). It also ends in a recommendation the Director can act on, which meets standard 5 (customer relevance).

### Weaknesses (most to least threatening to the conclusion)
1. **The inference does not follow from the evidence, and the key figure is internally inconsistent.** This misses standard 6, "Uses clear and logical argumentation": "Products should be internally consistent and acknowledge significant supporting and contrary information affecting judgments". It also misses standard 3, "state assumptions explicitly when they serve as the linchpin of an argument... and explain the implications for judgments if assumptions prove to be incorrect". It misses standard 4 as well, "identify and assess plausible alternative hypotheses".
   > "Contact-tracing coverage stands at 68%. Of 337 identified contacts, 289 have been reached. No secondary cases have been identified among reached contacts. We therefore assess that sustained community transmission beyond the index market cluster is unlikely."
   Why it matters, in four parts:
   - 289 of 337 is about 86%, not 68%. Either the percentage or the counts are wrong, or "coverage" means something the memo does not define. The reader cannot tell how many contacts were never reached, which is 48 on the counts or about 108 on the percentage.
   - The finding of "no secondary cases among reached contacts" is read as the absence of onward transmission. It could just as well reflect follow-up that is too short, or contacts still inside their monitoring window. It also says nothing about unreached contacts or about cases with no traced link.
   - The memo's own text points the other way: "a majority" of cases had market contact, so a minority did not, and that minority is never discussed.
   - Because the stated contact-tracing result does not bear on the question, the conclusion is unsupported rather than merely hedged.
   Fix: First reconcile 68% against 289/337 and define "coverage". Then state the linchpin assumption outright: "We assume contacts reached and followed for the full monitoring period would show onward cases if transmission were occurring." Add what follows if it is wrong. Then weigh two competing explanations against the same facts: (a) transmission is confined to the market cluster; (b) transmission is ongoing in the community and not yet detected, because of unreached contacts, cases with no market link, or surveillance lag. State what evidence would separate them, such as the proportion of cases with no market link, the follow-up duration of reached contacts, and the cases' dates of onset.

2. **The uncertainty is a single vague hedge, with no confidence statement and no basis given.** This misses standard 2, "Properly expresses and explains uncertainties associated with major analytic judgments". The library requires the likelihood of the event and the analyst's confidence in its basis, with causes of uncertainty noted and indicators that would change the level.
   > "...sustained community transmission beyond the index market cluster is unlikely."
   Why it matters: "Unlikely" is the only calibration the Director gets for a recommendation not to expand the response. It is ICD 203's 20–45% band (first set), which is too wide to separate "slightly doubtful" from "a real chance", and nothing in the memo says which end the Cell means. No confidence level or reason for confidence is given, and the memo names no sources of uncertainty (see weakness 1). The one clearly open risk, health workers, is hedged as "a concern" and is not assessed.
   Fix: Give a calibrated likelihood term with the reasoning behind it, in a separate sentence from the confidence statement. The library forbids putting likelihood and a confidence level in the same sentence. Example: "We assess that sustained community transmission beyond the market cluster is unlikely (20–45%). Our confidence is low-to-moderate, limited by incomplete contact follow-up and by cases with no known market link. Indicators that would raise the likelihood: cases with no market link, or any health-worker or household cluster." (The library does not define confidence levels, so state the basis rather than rely on the label.)

3. **The main message is not up front.** This misses standard 6, which asks for "a clear main analytic message up front".
   > "Over the past three weeks, 47 confirmed and 12 probable mpox clade Ib cases have been reported across Goma district."
   Why it matters: The memo opens with case counts and background. The judgment arrives in paragraph 2 and the recommendation in the last line, so a Director who stops early gets neither. It is a short memo and the judgment is clear once reached, so this ranks below the reasoning problems above.
   Fix: Open with "We assess that sustained community transmission beyond the Nyiragongo market cluster is unlikely; we recommend keeping the current surveillance posture", followed by the evidence and its limits.

I considered and dropped three candidates:
- The health-worker point is also unsupported. "No HCW cases have been reported" is not the same as none occurring, and "standard PPE protocols are in place" is an assumption, not an observed fact. It is handled in part by "remains a concern" and is not load-bearing for the conclusion, so I did not raise it as a separate finding. If you want a fourth item, this is the one.
- The memo does not say how it relates to prior analysis. Nothing in it shows a prior position being revised, so standard 7 is not scored.
- It has no visual information. Standard 9 is not required at this length.

Overall read: this is a clear, short and decision-oriented memo whose conclusion is not earned by its own evidence. It reports facts accurately and separates them from its inference, but the one numerical result it relies on is inconsistent, the key absence-of-evidence step is unexamined, and the confidence in the call is unexpressed. As written it should not support a decision not to expand the response.

### If I were the analyst, the one change I'd make first
Reconcile the contact-tracing figures (68% against 289/337) and rewrite the argument. Name the assumption that unreached and still-in-window contacts do not change the picture, state what follows if it is wrong, and set the "confined to the market cluster" explanation against "undetected ongoing community transmission". Then restate the judgment with a calibrated likelihood and a separate confidence statement.
